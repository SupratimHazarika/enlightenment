How to judge wellness-practice claims: Access Bars as a case study

Access Bars is a hands-on practice offered by the organisation Access Consciousness, which dates it to the 1990s. Its own pages make broad claims about releasing limiting thoughts and emotions. Here is how to read such claims.

Four questions

  1. Who is making the claim? Is it the organisation selling the service, or an independent source?
  2. What kind of evidence is it? A testimonial, a small pilot, or a randomised controlled trial (RCT) with a comparison group?
  3. How big and how controlled? The strongest study we read was a tinnitus RCT with 42 participants, and both groups improved.
  4. Did I read the study or only its abstract? Say which.
