Why 27 nakshatras and 12 signs line up every 120°

The ecliptic, the Sun's apparent yearly path, is 360°. Jyotish divides it in two ways at once. There are 12 rashis (signs) of 30° each, and 27 nakshatras (lunar mansions) of 360 ÷ 27 = 13⅓° each. That is 13°20′, because a third of a degree is 20 arcminutes.

Where the boundaries meet

A sign boundary falls at every multiple of 30°, and a nakshatra boundary at every multiple of 13°20′. They meet only where a longitude is a multiple of both. The least common multiple of 30° and 13⅓° is 120°: 120 ÷ 30 = 4 signs, and 120 ÷ 13⅓ = 9 nakshatras.

Padas

Each nakshatra has four padas of 3°20′. Since 30° ÷ 3°20′ = 9, every sign holds exactly nine padas. That is why the 108 padas (27 × 4) fit the 12 signs exactly.

Calculation versus interpretation: these divisions are arithmetic conventions, and you can check them. Traditional meanings attached to a nakshatra are a different kind of claim, and the arithmetic does not support them.
