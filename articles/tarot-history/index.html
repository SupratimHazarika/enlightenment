Where tarot really comes from (and why it isn't ancient Egyptian)

The earliest documented tarot decks appear in northern Italy between about 1440 and 1450. Allegorical picture cards called trionfi ("triumphs", the origin of "trumps") were added to the ordinary four-suit pack. For about three centuries tarot was a trick-taking card game, and it still is in France.

How fortune-telling arrived

The earliest evidence of tarot used for divination is a Bologna manuscript from about 1750. In the 1780s Antoine Court de Gébelin and the card-reader Etteilla promoted the idea that the cards encoded ancient Egyptian wisdom. Historians, including Michael Dummett, have shown there is no evidence for that.

The 1909 deck learners use

The Rider–Waite–Smith deck (London, 1909) gave every minor card a full illustrated scene, which is why beginners can read from the pictures. Waite himself called the divinatory meanings of the trumps "artificial and arbitrary… in the highest degree".

Myth check: "tarot is Vedic", "tarot is Egyptian" and "tarot is Kabbalah" are all unsupported. Tarot is a European card tradition.
